\section{Discussion and limitations}
The final controlled comparison supports a functional role for correct intermediate-state correspondence in this prepared matrix setting. The predicted-null intervention shows a pathway through which that benefit can reach the frozen second operator while preserving every first-step linear logit. The same-loss convergence diagnostic substantially narrows the original nonlinear advantage. Together, these findings show why first-step accuracy, state similarity, and usable composition must be measured separately.

The practical lesson is a sequence of checks: hold correct output supervision fixed when testing correspondence; test predicted intermediate states through the actual downstream computation; distinguish oracle repairs from available predictors; and adequately fit the affine family before interpreting a nonlinear advantage as expressivity. The unsuccessful null-only objective adds a boundary: identifying a useful subspace does not establish that supervising only that subspace is sufficient.

\paragraph{What the interventions identify.}
The readout-null projector concerns one fixed linear readout. It does not remove nonlinear answer information or all task information. Hybrid predictions may leave the encoder manifold. The correct/incorrect intervention changes state consistency with fixed soft teacher targets, so it is not an isolation of algebra from every possible output-related signal.
The source encoders already received correct single-generator preparation. Findings are conditional on those representations and do not establish spontaneous algebra acquisition.
The program supplies operation identity and order; the model predicts a state and answer, rather than discovering the requested composition rule.

\paragraph{Generalization and statistical limits.}
The final test separates complete finite-world orbits and excludes past examined test orbits, but training inputs may recur from earlier studies under new models.
Five source clusters are a modest replication count, and their corpora and test pairs are shared.
Bootstrap intervals are descriptive and not corrected for multiple hypotheses.
The matrix mechanism is not confirmed in the irreversible polynomial setting, where primitive generalization also fails.
Different domain architectures and supervision directions prevent causal attribution of domain differences to algebra alone.
Aggregate compound accuracy remains about 20\%, and paired double correctness is low; this is a local improvement, not robust hidden-relation inference.

\paragraph{Optimization and numerical limits.}
An additional functional expressivity advantage was not confirmed, but equivalence is also not established.
Direct affine fitting changes parameterization, precision, and budget while retaining the function family and empirical loss.
The ill-conditioned design and large coefficients leave numerical robustness and broader out-of-distribution stability unresolved.
LayerNorm imposes an affine constraint in exact arithmetic; the nominally full-rank design may exploit tiny finite-precision deviations from that constraint. The present checks do not isolate this possibility from other conditioning and optimization effects.
A finite-objective convergence bound does not bound held-out state error.
No exact percentage allocation to optimization, conditioning, or expressivity is supported.

\section{Conclusion}
Correct supplied state correspondences can improve held-out composition under matched output supervision. In the final prepared-encoder matrix experiment, predicted readout-null information transmits part of this functional advantage without changing first-step logits. Adequate same-loss affine fitting recovers most of the correspondence-sensitive gain, leaving no confirmed additional functional expressivity advantage. The contribution is a controlled account of this effect and its boundaries, rather than a universal claim that detectable algebraic geometry guarantees systematic inference.
